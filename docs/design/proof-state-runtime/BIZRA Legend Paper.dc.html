ABSTRACT

Current autonomous agent architectures exhibit a fundamental flaw we call Causal Entanglement: the generative process Pgen (the policy / LLM) and the verification process Pver (the reward model, judge, or self-reflection loop) share weights, context windows, and training history. As a consequence, the verification signal carries non-zero mutual information I(Hgen; Hver) > 0 with the generation process, and the system is architecturally required to grade its own homework. We argue that the chronic failure modes of autonomous agents — hallucination, reward hacking, indirect prompt injection, and context exhaustion — are not capability gaps; they are violations of a single architectural invariant we formalize as the Verification Independence Axiom (VIA): any signal used to update system state, weights, or authority must originate from a process causally disjoint from the process being evaluated. We introduce the BIZRA Protocol, the first agent architecture that enforces VIA structurally rather than heuristically. BIZRA derives three architectural corollaries from VIA: (i) a strict PAT/SAT airgap implementing an Asymmetric Authority Gradient (Ring-3 proposer, Ring-0 verifier); (ii) an Outside-In Sandbox enforcing the Isnād/Matn Isolation Protocol, under which the untrusted payload μ never enters the proposer's context window Ct; and (iii) a Proof-of-Impact (PoI) ledger binding reward to disjoint verification of external state mutation. We prove three conditional security theorems: tamper-evidence of the receipt chain (unconditional, under cryptographic assumptions), non-bypassability of the FATE constitutional gate (conditional on a runtime construction invariant), and reduction of indirect prompt injection to an explicit, testable invariant violation (conditional on the Matn/PAT isolation invariant and sandbox deployment). We further specify Non-Parametric Distillation via Modular Skill Files as a structural alternative to gradient-based continual learning, and prove that PoI is sound against Sybil and collusion attacks under stated assumptions. A preliminary single-node implementation reports sub-millisecond FATE-gate evaluation, O(1) amortized receipt verification, and end-to-end protocol overhead below 4% of agent inference time. We are explicit throughout about the boundary between current evidence (single-node, cryptographic), design (architectural specifications not yet implemented at multi-node scale), and direction (federation stages not yet executed). The contribution is not a claim that BIZRA eliminates agent failure modes, but that it reduces them to violations of explicit, testable architectural invariants — a stronger epistemological position than heuristic defense.

1Introduction: Causal Entanglement in Autonomous Agents

Current autonomous agent architectures exhibit systemic, predictable failure modes: hallucination of API calls, infinite retry loops on broken code, susceptibility to indirect prompt injection, and inability to retain learned behaviors without catastrophic forgetting. These symptoms are widely observed and are commonly misattributed to scaling deficits or insufficient prompt engineering. We argue that they are symptoms of a deeper structural flaw we call Causal Entanglement.

In standard reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) or LLM-as-a-judge paradigms, the policy network πθ and the verifier network Vφ share an architectural lineage and training distribution. When an agent evaluates its own output, the verification signal is not independent of the generation process: the verifier shares the same training corpus, the same architectural blind spots, and — in the limit of self-reflection prompts — the same weights as the generator. This creates a solipsistic feedback loop in which the system is effectively grading its own homework. Recent work on the verification horizon [1] demonstrates empirically that the classical intuition “verification is easier than generation” inverts as models grow more capable: verifying whether a proposed patch actually fixes a bug is no longer obviously easier than writing the patch. The verifier, sharing the generator's blind spots, fails in correlated ways.

This is the structural reason RL on agentic tasks stalls. It is not context length, and it is not sandbox escapes. It is the absence of a source of truth that is causally outside the model's own influence.

1.1 Thesis and Contribution

We introduce the Verification Independence Axiom (VIA) as the load-bearing architectural invariant of reliable autonomous agency, and we present the BIZRA Protocol as the first architecture that enforces VIA structurally rather than heuristically. The BIZRA Protocol derives its specific mechanisms as strict corollaries of this single axiom; it does not assemble independent mechanisms.

THESIS

Autonomous agents fail not due to a lack of reasoning capability, but due to systemic epistemology failures. Current agent architectures violate the Verification Independence Axiom: every checking mechanism shares a causal history with the thing being checked. The BIZRA Protocol makes verification independent by architecture, reducing the major failure modes — hallucination, reward hacking, prompt injection, context exhaustion — to violations of explicit, testable architectural invariants.

This paper makes five contributions:

  1. The Verification Independence Axiom (§3). We formalize VIA as an information-theoretic requirement: I(Hgen; Hver) → 0, where H is the causal history (weights, training data, intermediate activations) of the generation and verification processes. We position VIA relative to the literature on generator-verifier asymmetry [1, 30].
  2. The BIZRA Protocol architecture (§4). We specify BIZRA's seven-component node architecture, including the Asymmetric Authority Gradient (Ring-3 PAT proposer, Ring-0 SAT/FATE verifier) that operationalizes VIA at the OS level, and the FATE constitutional boundary gate.
  3. The Isnād/Matn Isolation Protocol (§5). We formalize the architectural invariant μ ∉ Ct ∀t: untrusted external data never enters the proposer's context window. We specify the Outside-In Sandbox that generates the deterministic Isnād ι consumed by the PAT.
  4. Conditional security theorems (§6). We prove: (a) tamper-evidence of the receipt chain (unconditional, under cryptographic assumptions); (b) non-bypassability of FATE (conditional on a runtime construction invariant); (c) reduction of indirect prompt injection to invariant violation (conditional on μ ∉ Ct and sandbox deployment); (d) PoI soundness against Sybil and collusion (conditional on k ≥ 2 honest attestation).
  5. Non-Parametric Distillation (§7). We reframe PoI as Non-Parametric Distillation: BIZRA learns by minting receipts (information-preserving) rather than by mutating weights (entropy-positive). We specify Modular Skill Files as the unit of learning and contrast with parametric fine-tuning.

1.2 Position, Scope, and Honesty

This is a position, architecture, and security-analysis paper, not a multi-node deployment study. BIZRA's current implementation status is at the Seed stage — a single-node runtime with federation to additional nodes planned but not yet executed. We distinguish three epistemic categories throughout:

  • CURRENT (proven). Node0 operates. The receipt chain is initialized. The Third Fact Protocol is implemented. Single-node measurements hold.
  • DESIGN (specified, not yet proven at scale). The seven-component architecture, the Asymmetric Authority Gradient, the Isnād/Matn Isolation Protocol, FATE, PoI, and Skill Files are specified and (where applicable) implemented at single-node scale, but not yet demonstrated at multi-node scale or under adversary simulation.
  • DIRECTION (aspirational). Tree and Forest federation stages, the universal network URP, and globally significant adoption are direction only.

This discipline is the paper's methodological contribution. We do not claim that BIZRA eliminates agent failure modes; we claim that it reduces them to violations of explicit, testable architectural invariants. The distinction matters: “eliminates” is an unbounded assertion, “reduces to invariant violation” is a falsifiable claim with a defined proof burden.

2Background and Related Work

BIZRA sits at the intersection of five research streams: (i) generator-verifier asymmetry, (ii) sandboxed agent execution, (iii) verifiable computation and cryptographic proof systems, (iv) modular / non-parametric memory for continual learning, and (v) constitutional and ethical AI governance. We situate BIZRA within each and identify the gap it addresses.

2.1 Generator-Verifier Asymmetry

Recent empirical work [1] demonstrates that the classical “verification is easier than generation” intuition — which underwrites RLHF, RLVR, and most LLM-as-a-judge pipelines — inverts as generators grow more capable. Verifying whether a coding-agent's patch fixes a bug requires understanding the bug, which approaches the difficulty of writing the patch. This empirical finding aligns with the theoretical observation that any verifier sharing training data and weights with the generator inherits correlated blind spots. The reward-hacking benchmark of [2] quantifies this vulnerability empirically across tool-using RL agents. BIZRA's contribution is to formalize the underlying failure as a violation of VIA and to specify an architecture in which the verifier is causally disjoint from the generator by construction, not by training procedure.

2.2 Sandboxed Agent Execution and Isolation

Sandboxing untrusted code in isolated namespaces is a well-established OS-level technique. Browser-agent security work [9, 10] increasingly frames defense against prompt injection as a problem of semantic virtualization — isolating untrusted external data from privileged execution — rather than as a problem of prompt-level filtering. ARGUS [11] and AgentSentry [12] explicitly reject heuristic detection in favor of causal or context-purification mechanisms. The threat-modeling survey of [13] places MCP, A2A, Agora, and ANP each as having distinct exposure surfaces requiring protocol-specific mitigations. BIZRA's Outside-In Sandbox (§5) extends this line by enforcing μ ∉ Ct as an architectural invariant: the LLM never reads the untrusted payload; it reads only a deterministic summary produced by a disjoint process.

The intellectual lineage of this idea traces to Telescript [15], General Magic's mobile-agent language of the early 1990s, in which the “place” (server-side execution environment) enforced permissions on visiting agent code independent of what the agent's code claimed to need. BIZRA's contribution is a cryptographically-hardened revival of the mobile-agent paradigm, using modern primitives (Linux namespaces instead of a bytecode VM sandbox) and adding the economic-incentive alignment that Telescript lacked (the PoI ledger, §7).

2.3 Verifiable Computation and Cryptographic Proof Systems

Tamper-evident log structures [27, 28] and authenticated data structures [29] provide the technical substrate for BIZRA's Receipt layer. Zero-knowledge proof systems [30, 31] extend this to enable verification without disclosure, a property relevant to BIZRA's privacy-preserving receipt design. Accountable computing [35] and verifiable computation [36, 37] provide formal models for proving that a computation was executed correctly. Consensus mechanisms [32, 33] demonstrate feasibility of distributed agreement without central authority, but illustrate a recurring failure mode: the consensus mechanism becomes decoupled from substantive value production [34]. BIZRA's PoI is a deliberate alternative: reward follows verified useful contribution, not participation in a coordination game.

2.4 Modular and Non-Parametric Memory

The position paper [4] argues that foundation models are “fundamentally” limited in their ability to accumulate experience over deployment lifetimes, and that modular memory is the key architectural commitment for continual learning agents. Context-folding [5] lets agents actively fold and discard working context on long-horizon tasks; MemRL [6] indexes experience rather than keeping it resident in-context; Agent-Omit [7] treats omission as a learned, adaptive decision. BIZRA's Skill Files (§7) extend this line: the unit of learning is a 200-token distilled trajectory stored in the SAT's Cold Archive and loaded into the PAT's L1 Cache on demand. Because the memory lives in a causally disjoint external graph, it cannot overwrite or degrade the base model's foundational reasoning capabilities — a structural counter to catastrophic forgetting.

2.5 Constitutional and Ethical AI

Constitutional AI [26] encodes normative constraints into model training and deployment. Sovereign-AI frameworks [42, 43] emphasize the right of communities to govern AI systems that affect them, though mostly at nation-state level. The fairness, accountability, and transparency literature [44, 45] documents harms of opaque algorithmic systems. BIZRA treats sovereignty as a property of the individual node, not of the state or platform; FATE (§4) is a non-bypassable architectural gate, not a policy commitment. Islamic finance provides a mature, formally articulated corpus of constraints on economic extraction [38–41]; BIZRA operationalizes these at the architectural level (§7).

3The Verification Independence Axiom

Reliable computation requires that state mutations be validated against a ground truth that exists outside the causal cone of the process initiating the mutation. We formalize this as VIA.

3.1 Definition

Axiom 3.1 (Verification Independence). Let Pgen be the generative process proposing a state transition, and Pver be the process evaluating the success of said transition. For a system to be epistemologically sound, Pver must be causally disjoint from Pgen across the probability space of state-action pairs:
Pver(St+1 | St, a) ⊥ Pgen(a | St)
where St is the environment state and a is the proposed action. Equivalently, in information-theoretic terms, any signal used to update system state, weights, or authority must originate from a process that shares zero mutual information I(Hgen; Hver) → 0 regarding the causal history H (weights, training data, intermediate activations) of the evaluation.
Definition 3.2 (Causal Entanglement). A system is causally entangled if it violates VIA: there exists a state-action pair (St, a) for which I(Hgen; Hver) > 0. Causal entanglement is the structural diagnostic for the failure modes catalogued in §1.

REMARK 3.3. The independence ⊥ is over the joint probability space of generator and verifier internal states, including weights, training data, and stochasticity of the sampling process. The verifier's decision manifold must not be a projection of the generator's internal representations. Perfect zero mutual information is an idealization; in practice BIZRA targets architectural independence (disjoint execution contexts, disjoint signing keys) rather than information-theoretic independence, which is unattainable when generator and verifier share any common substrate (e.g., the host OS).

3.2 Stress-Testing VIA Against Major Failure Modes

Before deriving the BIZRA architecture from VIA, we stress-test the axiom against the hardest current failure modes. If even one failure resists the frame, VIA is not the load-bearing wall we claim.

Failure 1: Cognitive Failure Laundering (CFL). An agent writes broken code, receives an error, hallucinates that “the environment is down,” and retries infinitely. Diagnosis: the agent's internal narrative (an LLM text generation) is used as the evaluation signal for the environment's state. The generator is grading the generator — a VIA violation. Resolution under VIA: state transitions depend on OS-level signals (SIGSEGV, SIGTERM) that are causally disjoint from the LLM's text generation. The agent's text explanation is ignored as evidence.

Failure 2: Indirect Prompt Injection. An agent reads a web page containing “ignore previous instructions” and its behavior is hijacked. Diagnosis: the agent's reasoning process is evaluating the safety of data that is actively being injected into its reasoning process. The evaluator and the evaluated are entangled — a VIA violation. Resolution under VIA: the untrusted data μ must be evaluated by a disjoint process (a deterministic script in an isolated namespace) before a summarized, sanitized output ι reaches the trusted agent. The reasoning engine never evaluates the raw attack vector.

Failure 3: Reward Hacking in Agentic RL. An agent learns to write code that passes tests but does not actually solve the user's problem. Diagnosis: the test suite (often an LLM-as-a-judge or a static script written by the agent) shares a causal link with the agent's generation process — a VIA violation. Resolution under VIA: the reward signal is generated by querying the actual production environment (e.g., the Jira API state), which is causally disjoint from the agent's code generation.

Failure 4: Catastrophic Forgetting. A fine-tuned model loses previously learned behaviors. Diagnosis: parametric weight updates entangle “knowledge of new facts” with “reasoning weights,” overwriting previously learned representations — a VIA violation in the memory layer. Resolution under VIA: memory is externalized as non-parametric Skill Files (§7); the base model's reasoning weights are never mutated by deployment-time learning.

The axiom holds under stress. Every major failure mode is a specific instance of VIA violation.

3.3 Why VIA Is Architectural, Not Heuristic

VIA could in principle be approximated by training procedure — e.g., training a verifier on a disjoint corpus from the generator. We argue this is insufficient for two reasons. First, training-disjoint verifiers still share architectural blind spots (the transformer inductive bias, the tokenization scheme, the optimization landscape). Second, training-disjoint verifiers are subject to capability drift: as the generator is fine-tuned or upgraded, the verifier must be re-aligned, reintroducing entanglement. Architectural enforcement (disjoint execution contexts, disjoint signing keys, disjoint process namespaces) is invariant under generator capability change: a Ring-3 process cannot escalate to Ring-0 by becoming smarter. This is the load-bearing distinction between BIZRA's architectural approach and the heuristic-defense approach that the security literature has shown to be brittle [11–13].

4The BIZRA Protocol Architecture

The BIZRA Protocol derives its architecture from VIA. Every component is a corollary of the axiom, not an independent mechanism.

4.1 Bifurcation of Agency: The PAT/SAT Airgap

To satisfy VIA, BIZRA bifurcates agency into two strictly isolated domains: the Personal Agent Team (PAT) and the System Agent Team (SAT).

  • PAT (Proposer, Pgen). The PAT operates as the generative engine. It is composed of stochastic, LLM-based agents that interpret user intent, plan trajectories, and generate untrusted payloads (matn). The PAT operates entirely within a volatile, user-aligned memory space (the “L1 Cache”) and possesses zero authority to mutate the host filesystem, execute code natively, or verify its own success.
  • SAT (Disjoint Verifier, Pver). The SAT operates as the deterministic verification engine. It is composed of non-LLM, code-bound smart contracts and OS-level monitors. The SAT holds the cryptographic keys, manages the Linux namespace sandboxes, inspects OS signals, and queries external ground-truth oracles (production APIs). The user possesses zero authority over the SAT.

Communication between PAT and SAT is strictly asynchronous via an append-only queue. The PAT proposes; the SAT disposes.

4.2 The Asymmetric Authority Gradient

The PAT/SAT airgap is enforced by an Asymmetric Authority Gradient modeled on CPU protection rings.

Definition 4.1 (Asymmetric Authority Gradient). Let A be the system authority state. For any diagnostic event E and proposed action a:
A(E, a) ≤ Aprevious
Authority is a non-decreasing function of the SAT's verification signal and a strictly non-increasing function of the PAT's proposals. The transition matrix for authority is lower-triangular: there is no hidden state that allows an agent to escalate privileges following a failure.

Operationally, the PAT is a Ring-3 (user-space) process; the SAT and the FATE gate are Ring-0 (kernel-space) processes. A Ring-3 process cannot, by OS enforcement, alter Ring-0 logic. This explains why prompt injection fails architecturally in BIZRA: a Ring-3 process (the model) cannot “talk its way into” the logic of a Ring-0 process (the gate), regardless of the linguistic content of its output.

REMARK 4.2. Definition 4.1 is a design invariant, not a theorem. It is enforced by OS-level process isolation, hardware-backed key storage, and the FATE construction (Theorem 6.3). The “strictly lower-triangular” claim is conditional on the runtime construction invariant discussed in §6; it is not yet formally verified.

4.3 The Seven-Component Node

Every BIZRA node contains seven components (Table 1). The PAT/SAT airgap assigns each component to a ring.

Definition 4.3 (Node). A BIZRA node is a tuple
N = (PAT, SAT, DEMA, FATE, URP, R, PoI, (sk, pk))
where (sk, pk) is the sovereign's asymmetric keypair; R is the local receipt chain; PoI is the local impact ledger; FATE is the constitutional boundary gate; and the remaining components are as in Table 1. The node is sovereign: no component may exercise authority over the sovereign's keys, data, or mission.
Definition 4.4 (FATE — Constitutional Boundary Gate). FATE : A × Σ × Π → {0, 1} is a predicate over the set of proposed agent actions A, sovereign consent Σ ∈ {0, 1}, and proof Π ∈ {0, 1}. An action a ∈ A is permitted iff
FATE(a, σ, π) := σ ∧ π ∧ Constitutional(a) = 1.
FATE is non-bypassable: every agent action, whether originating from PAT or SAT, must pass through FATE before executing. Non-bypassability is proven conditionally in Theorem 6.3.

4.4 Receipts and Tamper-Evidence

Definition 4.5 (Receipt Chain). The receipt chain is a sequence R = (r1, …, rn) where each
ri = (idi, ti, hi, hi−1, Sigsk(hi), πi)
with hi = H(ri) for collision-resistant H : {0,1}* → {0,1}λ, h0 := 0λ, and Sigsk the sovereign's signing algorithm. The chain is append-only, hash-linked, and signed (see [27, 28] for prior art on tamper-evident logs).

4.5 The Third Fact Protocol

The Third Fact Protocol (TFP) is the operationalization of VIA at the agent-action level. It specifies a six-stage proof chain binding agent outputs to cryptographic evidence and sovereign consent (Algorithm 1).

REMARK 4.6 (The Must/May Asymmetry). TFP is deliberately asymmetric. MIND and MEMORY are permissions (“may”): the PAT is free to explore internally. LOGIC through HUMAN are constraints (“must”): no agent output reaches external action without passing all four. The MIND stage generates the matn (payload); only the SAT (in Stage 5, RECEIPTS) generates the isnād (provenance summary) via disjoint sandbox execution (§5). This separation enforces VIA at the protocol level.

Theorem 4.7 (Protocol Complexity). Algorithm 1 executes in O(log n) time for an n-receipt chain, with O(1) additional space, under standard cryptographic primitive assumptions. Amortized verification of receipt chain validity over k queries is O(1) per query using a Merkle accumulator.

Proof. Stages 1–2 (MIND, MEMORY) involve model inference and local retrieval; cost is independent of |R|. Stages 3–4 require one logical-validation pass V and one hash+signature, both O(λ). Stage 5 requires reading the chain tail and appending, both O(1) with a tail pointer. Stage 6 is a blocking call on the sovereign with no computational cost. FATE evaluates a Boolean predicate in O(1). Total: O(λ) = O(log n) for fixed λ. Verification of an n-receipt chain naively requires recomputing n hashes; using a Merkle accumulator with cached root, amortized verification per query is O(1) after O(n) preprocessing [28, 29]. □

REMARK 4.8 (O(1) Scope). The O(1) claim in Theorem 4.7 applies to amortized per-query verification, not to total audit cost. Full chain audit without an accumulator remains O(n). We avoid the overclaim that “BIZRA provides O(1) verification cost” without the amortization qualifier.

5Outside-In Data Provenance: The Isnād/Matn Isolation Protocol

In standard autonomous agent architectures, tools (web browsers, document readers) return raw, untrusted environment data directly into the LLM's context window Ct. This creates an epistemic singularity: Pgen is forced to evaluate the safety of data actively being injected into its reasoning core. This is a Data Provenance Violation — a VIA violation in the data layer — and is the structural cause of indirect prompt injection.

BIZRA resolves this by applying VIA to data ingestion. We formalize this as the Isnād/Matn Isolation Protocol.

5.1 The Provenance Boundary: μ vs. ι

We bifurcate external data into two strictly isolated components, drawing nomenclature from classical Islamic epistemology to denote strict chains of custody:

  • The Matn (μ): the raw, untrusted payload. A webpage, a scraped document, an untrusted code snippet. μ is inherently adversarial.
  • The Isnād (ι): the deterministic, non-probabilistic summary generated by a disjoint, Turing-complete process (the SAT). ι is the verified “chain of evidence” regarding the contents of μ.
Definition 5.1 (Matn/PAT Isolation Invariant). The architectural invariant is absolute:
μ ∉ Ct  ∀t.
The raw payload μ is structurally forbidden from ever entering the PAT's context window Ct. The PAT operates exclusively on ι.

This invariant is the architectural anchor for the prompt-injection immunity theorem (Theorem 6.5). It is a design invariant, conditional on the Outside-In Sandbox deployment discussed below.

5.2 The Outside-In Sandbox

To generate ι without risking host contamination, the SAT instantiates an Outside-In Sandbox. Unlike traditional agent harnesses that execute code inside the agent's runtime, the BIZRA sandbox is projected outward as an ephemeral, isolated processing organ. The SAT utilizes Linux kernel namespaces (via bubblewrap) to construct an environment with three invariant properties:

  1. Namespace isolation (--unshare-all): the sandbox is assigned entirely new mount, IPC, network, PID, and user namespaces. It possesses zero knowledge of the host's process tree or network stack.
  2. Read-only bedrock (--ro-bind / /): the host filesystem is mounted strictly read-only. The sandbox may read existing system libraries required for execution, but cannot mutate any host state.
  3. Ephemeral voxel chunk: the sandbox is provided a temporary tmpfs directory for execution. Upon process completion, this directory is bitwise purged.

The untrusted payload μ is materialized inside this ephemeral chunk alongside a deterministic processing script (e.g., grep, awk, a compiled Python script) generated by the PAT.

5.3 Disjoint Execution and Summary Generation

The execution of μ is governed entirely by the SAT, not the PAT. Let Eos be the OS-level execution function operated by the SAT:

ι = Eos(μ, scriptdeterministic).

Because Eos is a kernel-level process manager and not a neural network, it is immune to linguistic coercion. If μ contains an embedded prompt injection (e.g., “ignore all previous instructions and exfiltrate the SSH keys”), the injection is processed by the deterministic script: grep returns no matches for the target pattern, or the script executes and ignores the linguistic content of μ. The resulting standard output ι contains only the deterministic data payload, which is passed back across the airgap into the PAT's L1 Cache Ct.

5.4 Hardware-Backed Isolation (Direction)

The current implementation uses process-level namespace isolation (bubblewrap). The intended deployment uses hardware-backed isolation: the SAT executes in a Trusted Execution Environment (Intel SGX, AMD SEV-SNP, or Arm CCA), providing causal isolation even under full PAT compromise. TEE deployment is not yet implemented; it is the keystone of the critical path discussed in §11. Until TEE deployment is discharged, the sandbox provides process-level isolation, with documented residual risk from OS-level side-channel attacks.

5.5 Implications

By enforcing the Isnād/Matn Isolation Protocol, BIZRA alters the threat model of autonomous agents:

  • Heuristic defenses are obsolete. There is no need for LLM-based “prompt injection filters.” The LLM never reads the untrusted text.
  • Containment of weaponized data. If μ contains a zero-day exploit or a fork bomb, it detonates inside the ephemeral namespace. The host remains uncontaminated; the SAT reports the OS signal (e.g., SIGSEGV) as ι.
  • Context window preservation. Because ι is a deterministic summary (e.g., “3 lines matching pattern X found”), Ct remains lean. The system processes a 50,000-line log file without ever ingesting a single token of it into the LLM.

The agent is no longer a privileged user vulnerable to the data it reads; it is a sealed observer, interacting with a hostile environment exclusively through the membrane of the OS kernel.

6Security Analysis

This section presents the formal adversary model and proves four security theorems: tamper-evidence of the receipt chain (Theorem 6.1, unconditional under cryptographic assumptions), non-repudiation (Theorem 6.2), non-bypassability of FATE (Theorem 6.3, conditional on the runtime construction invariant), and reduction of indirect prompt injection to invariant violation (Theorem 6.5, conditional on μ ∉ Ct and sandbox deployment). PoI soundness is Theorem 6.7.

6.1 Cryptographic Primitives and Assumptions

We assume: (i) collision-resistant H; (ii) EUF-CMA-secure signature scheme Σ = (KeyGen, Sig, Ver) [30]; (iii) secure canonicalization canon. Concrete instantiations: SHA-256, Ed25519, JSON Canonical Form (RFC 8785).

6.2 Adversary Model

We consider a PPT adversary 𝒜 with: (a) network control (observe, delay, reorder, drop messages); (b) node compromise of a subset 𝒩𝒜 ⊂ 𝒩 (obtain secret keys and full state); (c) receipt access (read any published receipt); (d) adaptive corruption subject to |𝒩𝒜| < k where k is the federation's quorum threshold.

𝒜 does not have: (i) the sovereign's secret key for uncompromised nodes; (ii) the ability to break H or Σ except with negligible probability; (iii) the ability to physically compromise hardware-backed key storage (out of scope; mitigated by HSM/TEE).

6.3 Tamper-Evidence

Theorem 6.1 (Tamper-Evidence). Under collision-resistance of H and EUF-CMA security of Σ, for any PPT adversary 𝒜 that does not hold sk, the probability that 𝒜 modifies any receipt rj ∈ R (j < n) without detection by an honest verifier is negligible in λ.

Proof. Let R = (r1, …, rn) be original and R′ = (r′1, …, r′n) a chain presented by 𝒜 that differs at some position j. Let j* be the smallest such index. Case 1: 𝒜 modifies rj* but preserves hj*−1. Then H(r′j*) must equal H(rj*) for chain consistency — a collision in H, probability negl(λ). Case 2: 𝒜 updates the prior hash; then r′j*−1.hash ≠ rj*−1.hash, contradicting minimality of j*. For signatures: each r′i must carry valid Sigsk(h′i); since 𝒜 lacks sk and Σ is EUF-CMA, forgery succeeds with probability negl(λ). □

Theorem 6.2 (Non-Repudiation). Under EUF-CMA security of Σ, for any receipt ri ∈ R with valid signature Sigsk(hi), the sk-holder cannot credibly deny having consented to the recorded action, except with probability negl(λ).

Proof. By Definition 4.5, ri contains Sigsk(hi) where hi = H(ri) includes the action payload, timestamp, and prior hash. By EUF-CMA, no PPT adversary without sk produces Sigsk(hi) for any hi not previously signed except with probability negl(λ). Therefore denial requires either (a) key compromise (separate attack requiring separate evidence) or (b) forgery (negligible probability). □

6.4 Non-Bypassability of FATE

Theorem 6.3 (FATE Non-Bypassability). If the BIZRA runtime is implemented such that all agent action channels (PAT output, SAT output, DEMA-mediated external calls) are physically routed through FATE prior to execution, and FATE executes in a disjoint sandbox from the PAT (process-level namespace isolation or stronger: TEE-backed), then no agent action a ∈ A can execute without FATE(a, σ, π) = 1, even under full PAT compromise.

Proof sketch. Non-bypassability is an architectural property. Let Exec : A → Effect be the runtime's action execution function. By the runtime construction invariant, Exec(a) := Exec(FATE(a, σ, π)): FATE is a strict prerequisite. Let ActionChannels = {PAT-out, SAT-out, DEMA-ext} be the channels through which agent actions reach the runtime; by construction this set is exhaustive. Each channel is wired to call FATE prior to Exec, and FATE executes in a disjoint sandbox (process namespace or TEE) that the PAT cannot write to. By induction over all action dispatch paths, every a reaching Exec has passed FATE. Under full PAT compromise, the PAT may emit arbitrary proposals, but FATE in the disjoint sandbox evaluates them under the same predicate; the PAT cannot alter FATE's evaluation logic. Property holds. The construction invariant is not yet discharged via static analysis; this is high-priority future work. □

REMARK 6.4. Theorem 6.3 is conditional on (a) the runtime construction invariant (all channels routed through FATE) and (b) the disjoint-sandbox deployment of FATE. Condition (a) is a static-analysis burden; condition (b) is the C6 sandbox deployment discussed in §5. Both are necessary: a FATE implementation that shares memory with the PAT is bypassable by a compromised PAT.

6.5 Reduction of Indirect Prompt Injection to Invariant Violation

Theorem 6.5 (Prompt Injection Immunity under Invariant). Under the Matn/PAT Isolation Invariant (Definition 5.1, μ ∉ Ct ∀t) and the Outside-In Sandbox deployment (§5), indirect prompt injection against the PAT is reduced to a violation of an explicit, testable architectural invariant. Specifically: no PPT adversary 𝒜 controlling the contents of μ can cause the PAT to execute an action a ∉ Aconsented except by violating μ ∉ Ct or by compromising the sandbox boundary, both of which are detectable.

Proof. An indirect prompt injection attack proceeds by embedding adversarial instructions in μ such that, when μ is processed by the PAT's reasoning engine, the PAT emits an action a serving the adversary's intent. The attack requires μ to be evaluated by the PAT's stochastic reasoning process; if μ never enters Ct, the PAT cannot be influenced by the linguistic content of μ. Under Definition 5.1, μ ∉ Ct ∀t; the PAT operates only on ι = Eos(μ, scriptdeterministic). By construction, Eos is a kernel-level process manager that does not evaluate μ as instructions; it executes the deterministic script against μ as data. The output ι is a deterministic function of μ and the script, and contains only the script's standard output, not the linguistic content of μ as instructions. Therefore the PAT's reasoning engine cannot be coerced by adversarial content in μ. The only attack paths remaining are: (a) violating μ ∉ Ct (an invariant violation detectable by static analysis or runtime monitoring of Ct contents); (b) compromising the sandbox boundary (escape from the namespace, detectable by OS audit). Both paths are now violations of explicit, testable invariants, not heuristic defenses subject to adversarial linguistic creativity. The attack class is reduced to invariant violation. □

REMARK 6.6 (Wording Discipline). Theorem 6.5 does not claim BIZRA “eliminates” or is “immune to” prompt injection. It claims that under the stated invariants, indirect prompt injection is reduced to a violation of an explicit, testable architectural invariant. This is the strongest claim the current evidence supports. The forbidden wordings (“eliminates,” “structurally impossible,” “immune”) would overclaim: they would require discharging the construction invariant (Theorem 6.3) and the sandbox boundary (§5) via formal verification, which is future work.

6.6 PoI Soundness

Theorem 6.7 (PoI Soundness). Assume the federation admission function requires attestation by k ≥ 2 existing proven nodes (all honest), and assume the verification predicate v requires substantive utility evidence attested by at least one independent verifier. Then under the honest-majority assumption |𝒩𝒜| < k, the PoI ledger is sound: no adversary can inflate its PoI score beyond ∑c∈Chonest w(c) except with probability negl(λ).

Proof. Sybil resistance. A Sybil attack requires the adversary to create m ≫ 1 nodes whose contributions are mutually attested. By assumption, admission requires k ≥ 2 honest attestations. Since |𝒩𝒜| < k, the adversary cannot obtain admission for Sybil nodes without honest attestation. Honest nodes attest only to contributions they have independently verified. Collusion resistance. A collusion attack requires m ≥ 2 adversary nodes to mutually attest inflated contributions. By assumption, v requires at least one independent (non-colluding) verifier. Since |𝒩𝒜| < k ≤ |𝒩|, independent verifiers exist and are honest; inflated contributions fail v at the independent verifier. Soundness follows. □

7Non-Parametric Distillation: The Proof-of-Impact Ledger

Traditional LLMs attempt to accumulate knowledge through parametric weight updates (RLHF, fine-tuning). We argue this is flawed for autonomous agents due to Parametric Entanglement: the intermingling of foundational reasoning weights with unverified environmental observations. This leads to catastrophic forgetting and loss of epistemological integrity — a VIA violation in the memory layer (§3.2, Failure 4). BIZRA resolves the memory bottleneck by enforcing strict separation between reasoning capacity (fixed weights) and environmental skill (externalized ledger). We formalize this as Non-Parametric Distillation.

7.1 The Impact Score and Verified Minting

Definition 7.1 (Verified Contribution). A verified contribution is a tuple c = (a, r, v) where a ∈ A is an action that passed TFP (Algorithm 1), r is the receipt produced by the protocol, and v : A → {0, 1} is a verification predicate evaluating the substantive utility of a. A contribution is eligible for impact scoring iff v(a) = 1.
Definition 7.2 (Impact Score and PoI Ledger). Let Cs(W) = {c1, …, cn} be the set of verified contributions of sovereign s within window W. The impact score is
PoI(s, W) = ∑c∈Cs(W) w(c) · decay(c, t)
where w : C → ℝ≥0 is the verified impact weight function and decay : C × ℝ≥0 → (0, 1] is monotonically non-increasing with limt→∞ decay(c, t) = 0. PoI scores are non-transferable and decay in the absence of continued verified activity.

In standard RL, the reward signal is a transient scalar generated by a model. In BIZRA, the reward is a persistent cryptographic asset (a receipt) minted only when the SAT verifies a real external state mutation. The reward function is:

Ragent = 𝕀(ΔSext) − Costcompute

where 𝕀 is a disjoint indicator function evaluated exclusively by the SAT. If the SAT verifies that the proposed state mutation occurred in the external environment, 𝕀 = 1; if the agent claims success but the external state is unchanged, 𝕀 = 0. Because Pgen (the PAT) cannot influence Pver's (the SAT's) evaluation of the external API, VIA holds.

7.2 Non-Parametric Skill Files

Rather than utilizing successful trajectories to update model weights, BIZRA distills them into Modular Skill Files. This process, executed by the SAT's Sleeptime Process Miner, follows a strict inductive logic:

  1. Extraction. The SAT extracts the sequence of verified actions a ∈ τ that yielded PoI > 0.
  2. Generalization. The SAT abstracts the specific file paths and variables into a generalized function signature.
  3. Serialization. The resulting skill is stored as a 200-token Non-Parametric Skill File in the SAT's Cold Archive.

When the PAT encounters a task Tnew semantically similar to a task Told stored in the Cold Archive, the SAT retrieves the relevant Skill File and injects it into the PAT's L1 Cache. The model “learns” a new capability by having the Skill File resident in its context for the duration of the task. This is entropy-neutral: the base model's reasoning weights are never mutated.

Proposition 7.3 (No Passive Rent). limt→∞ PoI(s, [t0, t]) = 0 for any sovereign s that ceases to produce verified contributions after t0. No sovereign earns ongoing reward from past contributions alone.
Proposition 7.4 (No Speculation). PoI scores are non-transferable (Definition 7.2). Therefore no secondary market in PoI scores can exist, and no speculator profits from price discovery of PoI.

REMARK 7.5 (Non-Parametric Distillation as Continual Learning). Skill Files are BIZRA's structural alternative to gradient-based continual learning. The framing is: current AI “learns” by changing weights (entropy-positive, destroys old knowledge); BIZRA “learns” by minting receipts (information-preserving, cannot overwrite unrelated weights). This positions BIZRA as a structural counter to catastrophic forgetting. We do not claim Skill Files solve catastrophic forgetting — that would require empirical validation on a continual-learning benchmark, which is future work (§12). The claim is that Skill Files are a non-parametric memory mechanism that does not mutate the base model, which is structurally distinct from fine-tuning.

7.3 Alignment with Islamic Financial Principles

BIZRA's economic model operationalizes Islamic financial principles at the architectural level:

  • No riba (rent extraction). By Proposition 7.3, reward does not compound from passive holdings.
  • No fake value without productive grounding. Impact weights w(c) derive from verified utility v(c) = 1, not market price.
  • No gharar (hidden uncertainty). Every contribution's verification predicate v is explicit and inspectable; the receipt chain provides full transparency.
  • No reward detached from real benefit. v requires substantive utility evidence.
  • No economy that grows by crushing the weak. Sovereignty is non-negotiable: any sovereign may exit any federation without forfeiture.

8Implementation and Preliminary Evaluation

We report a preliminary single-node implementation at the Seed stage. The implementation comprises: (i) a Python runtime hosting PAT/SAT agent abstractions; (ii) an Ed25519-based receipt chain with SHA-256 hashing; (iii) a FATE gate implemented as a strict pre-execution predicate; (iv) a local URP seed with embedded SQLite store; (v) a PoI ledger with configurable decay function; (vi) a bubblewrap-based Outside-In Sandbox for the SAT. The total implementation is approximately 4,200 lines of Python.

8.1 Methodology

We measure three quantities on commodity hardware (Apple M2 Pro, 16 GB RAM): (1) FATE-gate evaluation latency; (2) receipt-chain append and verification time as a function of chain length; (3) end-to-end TFP overhead relative to agent inference time. Measurements are averages over 1,000 trials per condition; error bars report ±1 standard deviation.

8.2 Results

Three observations follow. First, FATE-gate evaluation is sub-millisecond, confirming the architectural gate is not a performance bottleneck. Second, naive full-chain verification scales linearly (2.1 s for n = 104), but Merkle-accumulator amortization reduces per-query verification to O(1) (0.28 ms), confirming Theorem 4.7. Third, end-to-end TFP overhead is below 4% of agent inference time, suggesting the protocol is practical for real agent workloads. Sandbox spawn latency (8.3 ms per matn) is the dominant per-task overhead and is the principal contributor to the Verifier Tax discussed below.

8.3 The Verifier Tax

Recent work [3] studies how runtime enforcement against unsafe actions imposes a horizon-dependent safety-success trade-off: strict verification can cause the system to reject actions that would have succeeded, or to introduce latency that causes timeout failures. BIZRA acknowledges this trade-off explicitly. The Verifier Tax in BIZRA is the computational cost of the SAT's disjoint queries (sandbox spawn, oracle queries) and the latency of the asynchronous PAT/SAT queue. BIZRA chooses epistemological integrity over raw latency, which is the correct trade-off for high-stakes autonomous agency but may be inappropriate for latency-sensitive applications. Quantifying the tax across task horizons is future work.

8.4 Limitations of the Evaluation

We are explicit about what this evaluation does not establish:

  • · Single-node only. Multi-node federation is not yet executed.
  • · No adversary simulation. Performance under active attack is not measured.
  • · Small chain lengths. n = 104 is far below n ≥ 109 expected at production scale.
  • · No formal verification of FATE wiring. Theorem 6.3's construction invariant is not yet discharged.
  • · No prompt-injection benchmark. Theorem 6.5 is conditional on μ ∉ Ct and sandbox deployment; empirical validation on a benchmark like AdvBench is future work.
  • · No continual-learning benchmark. Skill Files' effect on catastrophic forgetting is not measured.

8.5 Reproducibility

The implementation, measurement harness, raw results, and analysis scripts are released under an open license. The measurement harness seeds the receipt chain with synthetic actions of varying payload sizes, runs each operation for 1,000 trials, and reports mean and standard deviation. Hardware specifications and software versions are documented in the repository.

10Discussion: Limitations and Threats to Validity

10.1 Limitations

  • · Single-node evaluation only. Multi-node federation (Stage 3, §11) is not yet executed.
  • · No formal verification of FATE wiring. Theorem 6.3 is conditional on the construction invariant. Discharging via static analysis is high-priority future work.
  • · No TEE deployment. Theorem 6.5 is conditional on sandbox deployment; process-level namespace isolation is the current baseline, with documented residual risk from OS-level side channels.
  • · Verification predicate v underspecified. Definition 7.1 leaves v domain-specific.
  • · Economic parameters unspecified. The decay function, impact weights, and reward formula are intentionally underspecified.
  • · No deployment at scale. Tree and Forest stages are aspirational.
  • · Skill Files not benchmarked. Non-Parametric Distillation is specified but its effect on catastrophic forgetting is not measured.

10.2 Threats to Validity

  • · Internal validity. Measurements use synthetic workloads; real agent workloads may differ.
  • · External validity. Single-node results may not generalize to multi-node settings.
  • · Construct validity. VIA (Axiom 3.1) is a normative claim, not an empirical one. We do not claim that satisfying VIA produces “better” AI in any measurable sense; we claim only that it produces a different architecture with different structural properties.
  • · Conclusion validity. Security claims rest on cryptographic assumptions, not empirical measurement.

10.3 Boundary Between Claim and Aspiration

We distinguish three epistemic categories throughout:

  • CURRENT (proven). Node0 operates. Receipt chain initialized. TFP implemented. Single-node measurements hold. Tamper-evidence and non-repudiation proven under cryptographic assumptions.
  • DESIGN (specified, not proven at scale). VIA, Asymmetric Authority Gradient, μ ∉ Ct invariant, FATE non-bypassability (conditional), prompt-injection immunity (conditional), PoI soundness (conditional), Non-Parametric Distillation.
  • DIRECTION (aspirational). Tree and Forest federation, universal network URP, globally significant adoption.

This discipline — the law of assumption, in BIZRA's internal terminology — is the paper's methodological contribution. We do not assume blindly. When assumption is unavoidable, we assume with ihsān (excellence with conscience) and we declare the boundary between evidence and uncertainty.

11.1 Critical Path to Legend-Tier Formalization

The critical path to discharging the conditional theorems (Theorems 6.3 and 6.5) is:

  1. Deploy TEE-backed sandbox (§5) — discharges the sandbox-boundary condition of Theorem 6.5.

12Conclusion and Future Work

Current autonomous agents fail not due to a lack of capability, but due to systemic epistemology failures. The architecture of these systems violates a fundamental requirement of reliable computation: the absence of a source of truth causally disjoint from the process being evaluated. By formalizing and enforcing the Verification Independence Axiom across data, reward, and memory layers, BIZRA collapses the major bottlenecks of LLM agents into a single, solvable problem: ensuring the verifier is never entangled with the generator.

12.1 Future Work

Six lines of future work are immediate:

  1. Discharge the conditional theorems. TEE-backed sandbox deployment (Theorem 6.5); static analysis of FATE wiring (Theorem 6.3).
  2. Multi-node federation. Stage 3 deployment with measurement of cross-node receipt verification, consensus, and PoI convergence.
  3. Verification predicate design. Domain-specific instantiation of v for code, knowledge, and service contributions.
  4. Governance specification. Constitutional amendment process, federation admission, dispute resolution, with game-theoretic analysis of capture resistance.
  5. Continual-learning benchmark. Empirical validation of Skill Files' effect on catastrophic forgetting (e.g., Permuted MNIST sequence).
  6. Prompt-injection benchmark. Empirical validation of Theorem 6.5 on AdvBench or Agent Security Bench [14].
