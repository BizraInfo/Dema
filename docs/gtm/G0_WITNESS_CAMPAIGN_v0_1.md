## 1. Campaign Overview

**Name:** The House That Can Say No
**Summary:** Introduce BIZRA to three named skeptics as a habitat whose architecture
has no lane for manipulation — and invite them to prove it or break it.

**Primary objective (SMART):** By 2026-08-16, obtain **≥1 independent verification** of
the Genesis proof artifacts, performed by a named ICP-0 evaluator on their own machine,
with founder vouching absent from the verification path.

**Secondary objectives:** ≥3 packs delivered and acknowledged · ≥1 documented defect
found by a witness (a real finding is a success, not a failure) · drain baseline
recorded for the Aug 2 operation · zero claim-discipline defects in anything published.

**Explicitly NOT objectives:** followers, signups, waitlist, stars, reach, coverage,
inbound volume. `D` — Every one of those would be a G1 metric measured at G0, and
optimizing for them is the failure mode the doctrine exists to prevent.

## 2. Target Audience

**Primary — ICP-0, the Skeptical Verifier.** A security reviewer, senior engineer, or
auditor-minded builder who can clone a repo and run a CLI, is professionally rewarded
for finding what's wrong, and has no stake in BIZRA succeeding.

- *Pain:* they are being asked to trust agentic systems whose vendors self-certify, with
  no artifact they can independently check.
- *Motivation:* being handed something falsifiable. Skeptics don't want to be sold; they
  want to be given a target.
- *Where they are:* local-AI and agent communities, security/verification circles, your
  own network's harshest critic. Not a marketing channel among them.
- *Buying stage:* none. They are not buying. That is the point.

**Secondary — ICP-1, the Burned Builder** (post-Ω1 only). Someone who ran an autonomous
agent, lost work or leaked a credential, and now distrusts the whole category. Not
contacted in this campaign.

**Tone:** a house, not a pitch. Quiet, exact, falsifiable. If a sentence would survive
being wrong, it's fine. If it needs to be believed, cut it.
