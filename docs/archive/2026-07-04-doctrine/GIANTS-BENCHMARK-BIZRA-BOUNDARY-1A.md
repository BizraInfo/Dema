## 2 · What the Counts Actually Reward — the anatomy, from their own rulebook

**The rubric (awwwards.com/about-evaluation):** Design **40%** · Usability **30%** · Creativity **20%** · Content **10%**. Jury ≥18; the 3 scores furthest from mean are auto-discarded; HM ≥ 6.5; SOTD = daily maximum. A second, separate **Developer rubric** (award ≥7): Semantics · Animations/Transitions · Accessibility · WPO (performance) · Responsive · Markup.

**Eight components the giants share (extracted across sources):**
1. **Custom everything** — juries recognize templates and AI-layouts instantly; bespoke code is the entry ticket.
2. **Motion as language, not garnish** — GSAP/scroll-driven choreography; the practitioner consensus: WebGL/3D "are tools — when they serve strategy, projects win; when used for effect, they don't."
3. **Three.js/WebGL mastery** — the dominant stack behind recent winners.
4. **Typography as structure** (the Obys/Adoratorio school proves restraint also wins).
5. **Performance as design constraint** — usability is 30%: a 5-second load kills a 10/10 visual; budgets set *before* design.
6. **Real content, integrated** — only 10%, but disqualifying when fake: lorem ipsum reads as "no content strategy."
7. **Small dense teams** — the 7-year champion runs ~30 people: craft density beats headcount.
8. **They publish their instruments** — Locomotive open-sourced its scroll engine; giants tool the field they win in.

**And the honest economics:** the award is itself a product — submission fees, PRO directory plans, jury prestige. A 5-day score by 18 strangers, then frozen; Awwwards even removes winners that later change. **The award measures a moment.**
